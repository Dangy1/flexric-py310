\section{Evaluation}
\label{sec:evaluation}

\subsection{Setup and Methodology}
\label{sec:eval-setup}

\textbf{Platform.} AppWeaver runs on a FlexRIC near-RT RIC with the FlexRIC emulated E2 node. Four RIC applications are real FlexRIC xApps exercised over E2: KPM monitoring (E2SM-KPM),
slice control (SLICE), transport control (TC) and an RC validation xApp (E2SM-RC); MAC, RLC, PDCP and
GTP monitors complete the paper registry of \awres{A}{batch}{cards} Agent Cards
(\awres{A}{batch}{executable} executable). The emulated E2 node returns synthetic KPM values, so this
part of the evaluation measures composition and execution, not network effect; the network effect is
measured separately on ns-3 (Sec.~\ref{sec:eval-twin}). For the registry-scale experiment the rApp
Directory adds 46 emulated rApp cards (16 families, three fictional vendors, deprecated releases and
distractor cards) served by one process that reads live KPM from the KPM bus.

\textbf{Planner.} The planner model, sampling settings (temperature 0.2, seed 7), reasoning mode,
prompt hashes and LLM-host hardware are recorded in every batch manifest. This draft uses
\texttt{\awres{A}{batch}{model}} as the planner. Planner and observation caches are off
(\awres{A}{langgraph_agentcard}{planner_cached_records} cached planner calls).

\textbf{Benchmark.} Batch~A uses 50 operator intents: \awres{A}{langgraph_agentcard}{known_intents}
match one of the portal's three workflow templates (\emph{known}) and
\awres{A}{langgraph_agentcard}{open_world_intents} have no template (\emph{open-world}); 6 carry a
seeded conflict (another workflow holds the application the intent needs). Batch~B extends the set to
80 intents and adds \awres{B8}{langgraph_agentcard}{unseen_capability_intents} \emph{unseen-capability}
intents that need an rApp capability absent from every template and fallback rule. Each intent carries
its labelled chain and, where a written rule allows it, accepted alternatives; labels and rules are
released with the code.

\textbf{Methods.} \emph{Rule router} (keyword rules to the three templates); \emph{Single app} (the
best-matching application only); \emph{LLM (no catalog)} (the planner without the registry; names
that match no card are counted as hallucinated references); \emph{LLM (catalog)} (the planner given
the full catalog and the templates); \emph{AppWeaver (no repair)} (Agent-Card discovery and
composition with compatibility checks, without the repair/recovery step); and \emph{AppWeaver}.
Every (intent, method) pair is run \awres{A}{batch}{repeats} times.

\textbf{Metrics.} \emph{Accuracy}: the generated chain equals an accepted chain in order;
\emph{set accuracy} ignores order. A \emph{tool call} counts only if the RAN or rApp
\emph{acknowledged} it (E2 CONTROL-ACK, KPM indication, rApp reply) within 12\,s, not if the process
merely started. For seeded conflicts we report the share \emph{refused} before control was issued
and the share \emph{recovered} (the method reached a clean execution on its own), and the
false-positive rate on unseeded intents. \emph{Latency} is intent-to-launched-plan wall clock.
Rates carry 95\% confidence intervals (intent-clustered bootstrap; Wilson for conflicts).

\subsection{Twin Fidelity}
\label{sec:eval-twin}

\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.8\textwidth]{figures/fig_twin_validation}
\caption{Twin-predicted vs.\ measured relative KPI change of the deployed policy, one point per seed
pair. Top: twin with \awres{T}{batch}{twin_ues} UEs, live with \awres{T}{batch}{live_ues}; bottom:
load-calibrated twin (\awres{TL}{batch}{twin_ues} UEs). Dashed: perfect prediction.}
\label{fig:twin}
\end{figure*}

\textbf{Protocol.} The twin is an ns-3/5G-LENA network (ns-O-RAN: one LTE anchor,
\awres{T}{batch}{n_gnb} gNBs at 3.5\,GHz) behind its own FlexRIC instance; the live network is a
second instance with an independent seed (user drop, mobility, fading) and, in the first experiment,
25\% more users than the twin knows of. For each of three RC-actuated workflows composed by AppWeaver
-- energy saving (cell switch-off), traffic steering and QoE-driven steering (handover) -- the rApps
decide a cell-level policy on the twin's KPM, and the same policy is deployed on live. Each KPI change
is measured against a no-op run of the same instance, over \awres{T}{batch}{pairs} seed pairs.
Repeating a seed changes PRB use by at most 0.3\% and energy and throughput by at most 0.01\%,
so twin--live differences are not simulator noise. Transport and slice workflows cannot be checked
this way: ns-O-RAN exposes no TC or slicing control.

\textbf{Results.} With the twin's load matched to live, the twin predicts the energy effect of switching
cells off within \awres{TL}{energy_saving}{energy_j_per_s_mean_abs_rel_error_pp} percentage points
(\awres{TL}{energy_saving}{energy_j_per_s_mean_predicted_rel_pct}\% predicted,
\awres{TL}{energy_saving}{energy_j_per_s_mean_actual_rel_pct}\% measured) with the correct sign in
\awres{TL}{energy_saving}{energy_j_per_s_sign_agreement}\% of pairs, and the direction of the PRB change
in \awres{TL}{energy_saving}{prb_used_dl_sign_agreement}--\awres{TL}{traffic_steering}{prb_used_dl_sign_agreement}\%
of pairs. When the twin underestimates the load, the PRB direction is right in only
\awres{T}{energy_saving}{prb_used_dl_sign_agreement}\% of energy-saving pairs: an under-loaded twin has
spare capacity that live does not. Throughput changes are not predicted reliably (sign agreement
\awres{TL}{energy_saving}{dl_throughput_mbps_sign_agreement}--\awres{TL}{traffic_steering}{dl_throughput_mbps_sign_agreement}\%):
they depend on which users move, and the twin does not know where the live users are -- it switched
off cells that were empty in the twin but served users in live. A twin that matches the live network's
configuration and load is therefore a sound guard for cell-level policies (it rejects, e.g., sleeping a
loaded cell) and predicts their energy effect; predicting per-user QoS would require ingesting live UE
state from O1/E2, which we leave to future work.
